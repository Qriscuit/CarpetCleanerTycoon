## The idea

**An ordinary-looking dirty rug is hiding a fox. Your brushing brings it back.**

The viewer should want to make the next clean stroke and see the whole design. The clean reveal supplies the payoff, then the other two patterns give that feeling somewhere to go. This is a creative hypothesis about discovery and satisfying control, not a claim that the cut has already proved effective.

The first 15 seconds should communicate one promise: brush away the dirt and uncover something worth restoring. The wider brush earns a separate 25-second cut. Keep the original v1 progression concept as that longer version; its obsolete two-pass timing and absent-art assumptions are superseded by the present game.

## Three opening options

All variants keep 2.00–15.00 unchanged. Each is a different source opening, not just a different caption pasted onto the same footage.

- **A — discovery, recommended:** “Find the fox.” A late pass uncovers the eye/muzzle. Tests anticipation of the hidden design.
- **B — material satisfaction:** “That clean stripe.” A tight, high-contrast edge moves with the brush; the animal is secondary. Tests the pleasure of cause and effect.
- **C — completion:** “One spot left.” A cold open on a nearly restored Fox with one obvious residual patch. Hard-cut at 2 seconds into the cleaning montage. It is an outcome-first cold open, not continuous chronology. Tests a visible unfinished goal.

The animatic can switch these captions to review type size. Its alternate hook pictures are placeholders based on the current framing references; **all three moving opening takes remain to be recorded**. Pick the strongest continuous performance before fine-tuning copy. Do not select a winner from the animated stills alone.

## Vector art direction

Use the existing game's cream, mint and dark-green palette, with restrained fox-orange/gold accents. Bold, rounded plates should make four or five words readable at phone size. The artwork frames the transformation rather than adding sparkles, fake dirt, a drawn finger, fake buttons inside the game, or a replacement illustration of the rug.

`vectors/` holds full-canvas 1080 × 1920 graphics for drop-in alignment. Live-text versions retain editable copy; outlined versions render consistently without distributing font files. The end card is an opaque editorial graphic with a reserved live/still gameplay insert. The other caption plates are transparent overlays. `safe_guide.svg` is review-only and never belongs in an export.

The internal working region is x96–900 / y300–1220. It is not an official universal safe zone. Check essential text, the face and brush contact against the actual caption/CTA overlays in each platform's preview. The surrounding rug and floor may fill the frame. ## Sound direction

Keep the cloth sound close and soft. Use live contact audio for the brush and the existing completion sound at its real moment. No voice-over is needed for the first cut. Lay one continuous, quiet phrase from the game's original music under the edit; do not double it with music already baked into recorded takes. The existing system has no vacuum, purchase, coin or wet-tool sound assets, so those are not assumed deliverables. [Exact files and verified formats](Asset_Inventory.md).

The visual preview is intentionally silent: still images cannot demonstrate contact-synchronised sound. The asset inventory includes audio audition links; final balance and sync belong to the motion recording pass.

Before recording, approve the readable idea: does the first two seconds show what the hand does; does the final fox feel worth uncovering; and does the ending explain which game to play? Those questions are more useful here than promising a conversion rate.
